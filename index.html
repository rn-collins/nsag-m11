Medical Technology & Evidence Standards Governance

12 questions  ·  6 dimensions  ·  8–10 minutes

The governance of emerging health technologies — AI diagnostic tools, medical devices, digital therapeutics, neurotechnology — requires evidence standards that the current FDA regulatory framework was not designed to evaluate at scale. Organizations face a governance gap: tools move faster than evidence, evidence faster than regulation.

Question 1 of 12

This assessment scores your organization across six dimensions of medical technology & evidence standards governance. Each question uses a five-point scale. Your tier report is generated immediately after you submit your email — no waiting, no sales call required.

For: Health systems evaluating AI tools · Health insurers · Digital health companies · IRBs · Academic medical centers

Q1 of 12
Evidence Evaluation Capacity

Does your organization have a formal committee or process for independently evaluating the evidence supporting new health technologies before adoption — separate from vendor presentations?

Q2 of 12
Evidence Evaluation Capacity

Can your organization identify who conducted the validation studies for the most recently adopted AI or digital health tool, and whether those studies were independent of the manufacturer?

Q3 of 12
Regulatory Literacy

Can your clinical or administrative leadership distinguish between FDA clearance (510(k)), De Novo authorization, and PMA approval — and articulate what each implies about evidence reliability?

Q4 of 12
Regulatory Literacy

Does your organization have designated staff with training in Software as a Medical Device (SaMD) classification and the FDA's Digital Health Center of Excellence guidance?

Q5 of 12
Adoption Decision Infrastructure

Does your organization have a written protocol for technology adoption decisions that includes minimum evidence thresholds, equity impact assessment, and a structured reversal or sunset process?

Q6 of 12
Adoption Decision Infrastructure

Has your organization declined or paused adoption of a health technology in the past three years based on insufficient evidence — and is that decision formally documented?

Q7 of 12
Patient Communication Standards

Does your organization have a standard for informing patients when an AI system has contributed to a clinical recommendation, diagnosis, or care plan?

Q8 of 12
Patient Communication Standards

Can patients at your organization opt out of AI-assisted decision-making in their care, and is this option documented in your informed consent infrastructure?

Q9 of 12
Post-Adoption Monitoring

Does your organization have a system for attributing adverse events to specific digital health tools or AI systems — rather than attributing them solely to clinical error?

Q10 of 12
Post-Adoption Monitoring

Are technology performance metrics reviewed at defined intervals after adoption, with a documented threshold that would trigger suspension, retraining, or removal?

Q11 of 12
Institutional Ethics Integration

Does your organization's ethics infrastructure address AI-specific concerns — bias, opacity, training data provenance — as distinct categories from traditional bioethics review?

Q12 of 12
Institutional Ethics Integration

Has your organization conducted an equity audit of a technology currently in clinical use to assess differential performance across patient demographics, including race, language, and insurance status?
